## Architecture advantages and compromises

**Threadripper A/C:** more PCIe connectivity, superior multi-GPU platform, ECC RDIMM, quad-channel memory, higher memory capacity and stronger long-term expansion; higher price/power and unavailable matched RAM. **AM5 B:** lower platform price/power, better gaming-focused CPU, cheaper motherboard/RAM/case as architecture (current UDIMM price high), white showcase design, substantial 128GB capacity. Compromises: dual-channel memory, preferred 128GB, CPU-fed x8/x8 electrical dual GPU, fewer PCIe lanes, chipset/USB4 M.2 sharing, weaker 256GB+ expansion, stock HAVN VGPU not two-GPU-ready. **C:** Threadripper PCIe/expansion platform, potential lower acquisition cost, 48GB aggregate physical VRAM, one software-distributed model or two independent 24GB workloads; used-card condition/warranty risk, separate VRAM devices, software/communication overhead, ~700W GPU heat, older Ampere Tensor/RT/media features, incomplete fit-validated matching-pair evidence. One 5090 has 32GB on a single device and simpler software; two 5090 are not unified either.
