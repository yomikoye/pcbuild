## Architecture advantages and compromises

**Threadripper A/C:** more PCIe connectivity, superior multi-GPU platform, ECC RDIMM, quad-channel memory, higher memory capacity and stronger long-term expansion; price, power and exact-board/RDIMM supply disadvantage. **AM5 B:** lower platform price/power, better gaming-focused CPU, cheaper motherboard/RAM/case in architecture (though current UDIMM ask is high), white showcase design, substantial 128GB capacity. Compromises: dual-channel memory, 128GB preferred, x8/x8 electrical dual GPU, fewer PCIe lanes, chipset/USB4 M.2 sharing, weaker 256GB+ expansion and selected HAVN VGPU not two-GPU-ready in stock layout. **C:** same Threadripper expansion, potentially lower acquisition cost, 48GB aggregate physical VRAM and one software-distributed model or two independent 24GB workloads; condition/warranty risk, separate VRAM devices, inter-GPU software/communication overhead, ~700W GPU heat, older Ampere Tensor/RT/media features and incomplete fit-validated matched-pair evidence. For single-device latency, 5090 remains distinct from capacity/parallel-throughput aims; no aggregate memory-bandwidth-as-one-device claim.
